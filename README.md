## 🧭 Overview

Endgame Atelier is a study companion built for chess players who suspect that the final phase of the game deserves more respect than it usually receives. Instead of overwhelming a learner with thousands of positions pulled from grandmaster archives, this project narrows its focus to the humble, foundational endings — the king-and-pawn dances, the rook-versus-pawn standoffs, the bishop-and-knight duets that decide so many tournament results.

Think of it as a woodworker's bench rather than a lumberyard. You bring curiosity; the Atelier provides the chisels, the sandpaper, and a kindly voice telling you when your grip is off.

The tool runs entirely inside your browser session and does not require an account, a subscription, or any online identity. Your progress lives wherever you decide to keep it.

## 🎨 Origin Story & Philosophy

Every chess library contains a dusty shelf of endgame manuals. They are wonderful, and they are also intimidating. Beginners open them, see a diagram labelled "Lucena Position, Variation C4," and quietly close the book.

Endgame Atelier reverses that experience. It starts from the belief that foundational finales are not a technical footnote — they are a form of literacy. The ability to nurse a pawn to promotion with nothing but a king and a rook is the same literacy that lets you read a whole game honestly, without depending on tactical magic.

This repository is a reimagining, an homage, and a fresh workshop built alongside the spirit of earlier trainers. It is not a fork in the aggressive sense; it is a respectful conversation.

## 🎓 Interactive Training Modes

The heart of the Atelier is a set of training chambers, each devoted to a family of endings.

### Mode: Pawn Promotion Drills 🥇
A focused chamber where a lone pawn marches toward glory (or tragedy). You learn the rule of the square, the concept of opposition, and the delicate business of shepherding a candidate forward.

### Mode: Rook Against Pawn ⚔️
Perhaps the single most common practical ending in club play. This chamber drills the subtle dance of check, retreat, and blockade — the moves that separate a draw from a loss.

### Mode: Queen Versus Pawn 👑
Learn how a queen handles a dangerously advanced passer. The chamber teaches patience, geometry, and the correct moment to force the king into the corner.

### Mode: Bishop and Knight Duet 🎭
The classic mating sequence that has humbled countless improvers. Broken into stages so the pattern can be absorbed without panic.

### Mode: Two Bishops Symmetry 🧩
A companion chamber emphasizing coordination of the light and dark squares.

### Mode: Theoretical Draws 🕊️
Some positions are worth saving, not winning. This chamber celebrates the art of holding on with the barest of resources.

### Mode: Survival Scenarios 🌫️
Randomized starting frames that test whether the pattern has truly internalized.

## 📱 Responsive Interface Design

Some players study on a large desktop monitor with three windows open. Others steal five minutes on a phone during a commute. The Atelier refuses to punish either habit.

The layout adapts fluidly:
- On wide screens, the board and move list sit in comfortable parallel columns
- On tablets, controls become generous touch targets
- On narrow phones, the board remains full-bleed and readable without pinch-zoom gymnastics

The typography stays legible at every breakpoint, and interactive areas respect the 44-pixel comfortable-touch guideline wherever practical.

## ⚙️ Performance & Reliability Notes

Endgame Atelier is deliberately small in its runtime footprint. The rendering pipeline avoids unnecessary repaints, the position generator uses a bounded search so it can never wander into an infinite loop, and the training state is saved with a debounce so your device's storage is not thrashed.

The tool is built to survive interruption. If you close the tab mid-drill, the Atelier remembers where you stood. If your device runs out of battery during a session, the last stable snapshot of your progress remains safe.

## 🛠️ Configuration & Personalization

A short menu of preferences lets you sculpt the Atelier to taste:

- Board theme: classic, wood, marble, or monochrome
- Piece set: traditional, minimalist, or high-contrast
- Hint intensity: silent, whispered, or explicit
- Coordinate labels: always on, always off, or hover-only

Each preference is stored alongside your progress and can be reset to defaults from a single control.

## ❓ Frequently Considered Questions

**Does the Atelier require an internet connection?**
Only for the initial fetch of the app shell. Once loaded, drills run locally.

**Can I import my own positions?**
Yes. A simple textual format covers the vast majority of cases.

**Is there a scoring system?**
There is a progress view, but the Atelier treats accuracy and pattern recognition as more meaningful than a score.

**Will my data be uploaded anywhere?**
No. Progress remains on your device unless you intentionally export it.

**Can instructors use this in classrooms?**
Absolutely. The worksheet generator roadmap item exists specifically for that purpose.
